## Q1. Does the Copyright Ordinance, 1962 exclude judgments from copyright, or allow them to be reproduced?

### Takeaway
Judgments are not outside copyright. A judgment of a court falls within the statutory definition of a "Government work", and the Government is its first owner. However, s.57(1)(q)(ii) says that reproducing or publishing "any judgment or order of a Court, tribunal or other judicial authority" is not infringement, unless the court prohibits it. In practice the raw text of a judgment can be lawfully reproduced and published. The exemption does not extend to material added by publishers.

### Cited Findings (raw facts and quotes)
- The WIPO text is the Ordinance "as amended by Copyright (Amendment) Ordinance, 2000 dated 29th September, 2000." — [WIPO Lex](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/129350)
- **s.57(1)(q)(ii)** (paraphrased, with the exact fragment in quotes): reproducing or publishing "any judgment or order of a Court, tribunal or other judicial authority" is not infringement, unless the court or authority prohibits it. — [WIPO Lex](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/129350)
- **s.57(1)(q)(i)**: reproducing matter "published in any official Gazette", and reports of committees and commissions, is not infringement unless the Government prohibits it. — [WIPO Lex](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/129350)
- **s.57(1)(c)**: reproduction "for the propose [sic] of a judicial proceeding" or "for the purpose of a report of judicial proceeding" is not infringement. — [WIPO Lex](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/129350)
- **s.57(1)(a)**: fair dealing for "research or private study" and for "criticism or review, whether of that work or of any other work" is not infringement. **s.57(1)(b)**: fair dealing for "reporting current events" is not infringement. — [WIPO Lex](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/129350)
- **s.2(m) "Government work"**: a work "made or published by or under the direction or control of" the Government or its departments, *or any court, tribunal, or judicial or legislative authority in Pakistan* (the italicised part is paraphrased by the fetch tool). — [WIPO Lex](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/129350)
- **s.13(d)**: for a Government work, "Government shall, in the absence of any agreement to the contrary, … be the first owner of the copyright therein." — [WIPO Lex](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/129350)
- **s.22(1)**: where Government is the first owner, copyright subsists "until fifty years from the beginning of the calendar year next following the year" of first publication. — [WIPO Lex](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/129350)
- The Ordinance defines a court to include "any court, tribunal or other judicial or legislative authority in Pakistan" (search-snippet summary). — [WIPO Lex](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/129350)
- A 2026 practitioner guide says "Pakistani law recognises the concept of fair use under Section 57 of the Copyright Ordinance" (snippet only). — [Chambers GPG 2026](https://gpg-pdf.chambers.com/Trade-Marks-and-Copyright-2026/379/)

### Inferences (analysis)
- Because of s.2(m) and s.13(d), the State owns copyright in judgments. Section 57(1)(q)(ii) then makes their reproduction and publication non-infringing. A product may therefore collect, store, index and display the **text of judgments and orders** as issued by the court. It should take that text from court sources, such as court websites, certified copies or the Sindh High Court portal, rather than from publishers' copy-edited versions.
- The exemption has a proviso: it does not apply where the court has prohibited reproduction. The product therefore needs a process for spotting court orders that restrict publication, such as in-camera, sealed or anonymised matters.
- Section 57(1)(q)(ii) speaks of "reproduction or publication". It does not clearly cover *adaptation*, which includes translation (for example into Urdu) and abridgement. AI-generated summaries and Urdu translations probably rest on fair dealing (s.57(1)(a), research or review) or on the view that a summary in new words does not reproduce a substantial part. That view is reasoned, not tested in court, so treat it as uncertain.
- Gazette and Commission material (s.57(1)(q)(i)) can also be reproduced, which covers statutes published in the Gazette. Pakistan Code's own site, however, claims copyright for the Ministry ("© Copyright 2022 - Ministry of Law and Justice") and says its content is "currently under review" — [Pakistan Code](https://pakistancode.gov.pk/english/).
- s.57(1)(q)(ii) closely matches India's s.52(1)(q)(iv), which suggests Indian case law on the point is especially persuasive in Pakistan. That is my inference, not a sourced claim.

### Gaps
- I did not obtain the Ordinance text from pakistancode.gov.pk or ipo.gov.pk. The WIPO text is as amended to 2000, and I have not checked any later amendments to s.57 (for example any made after the IPO Pakistan Act 2012). Verify against the official current text before relying on clause numbering.
- The fetch tool's quote limit means the full wording of s.57(1)(q), including its exact prohibition proviso, was paraphrased rather than quoted in full. Re-extract it verbatim from the WIPO page or an official PDF (for example https://download1.fbr.gov.pk/Docs/2024220102483998427.pdf, found in search results but not fetched).
- I found no Pakistani judgment that interprets s.57(1)(q)(ii).

## Q2. Copyright in value added by law reporters (headnotes, editorial notes, citations, pagination, compilation)

### Takeaway
I found **no reported Pakistani decision** on whether copyright subsists in law-report headnotes or editorial additions. Under the general principles of the Ordinance, headnotes, editorial notes and selection or arrangement are likely protected as original literary works or compilations. The leading persuasive authority is Indian: *Eastern Book Company v. D.B. Modak*, which is comparative only. The one current Pakistani dispute involving PLD Publishers concerns the completeness of reported judgments (omitted footnotes), not copyright.

### Cited Findings (raw facts and quotes)
- **Pakistani dispute (not copyright):** On 19 Oct 2025 Dawn reported that a Peshawar High Court bench (Justice Ijaz Anwar and Justice Mohammad Naeem Anwar) directed the federal and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa law secretaries and the chief executive of PLD Publishers to file comments within a fortnight. The petition, filed by Barrister Asadul Mulk, seeks orders "rectifying the omission of footnotes from judgements and orders it publishes in law reports". It relies on the Law Reports Act 1875 (ss.3, 5, 6), which the petitioner says envisages publication of superior court judgments rather than "partial or selective publication", and on Article 94 of the Qanun-e-Shahadat Order 1984, under which law reports carry a "presumption of correctness". The petitioner called PLD Publishers (established 1947) "the largest legal-publishing house in the country". These are the petitioner's submissions, not findings of the court. — [Dawn, 19 Oct 2025](https://www.dawn.com/news/1949786)
- **Comparative, India (NOT Pakistani law):** *Eastern Book Company v. D.B. Modak*, Supreme Court of India. According to a search-engine summary (snippet only; the primary source could not be fetched), it held that "the judgments themselves cannot be copyrighted as they are public domain documents, while the preparation of headnotes involves skill and creativity, thus copyright subsists in the headnotes", applying a "minimum creativity" standard. — [Supreme Court of India PDF (inaccessible: ECONNREFUSED)](https://api.sci.gov.in/jonew/judis/30019.pdf); [Indian Kanoon (inaccessible: ECONNRESET)](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1062244/)
  - From my background knowledge, not verified in this session: the judgment is usually cited as (2008) 1 SCC 1 and was decided in December 2007. It is also commonly reported to have extended protection beyond headnotes and footnotes to the publisher's own paragraph numbering and editorial division of judgments. **Verify before citing.**
- A search summary described "a Sindh High Court ruling" that upheld a temporary injunction protecting editorial inputs in the "Supreme Court Cases" law reports. The linked source was a Delhi High Court 2001 page (latestlaws.com "2001-latest-caselaw-343-del"). This is very probably the Indian case *Eastern Book Co. v. Navin J. Desai* (Delhi HC, 2001), mislabelled as Sindh. **Treat it as unverified and do not cite it as Pakistani.** — [latestlaws (snippet only)](https://www.latestlaws.com/judgements/delhi-hc/2001/march/2001-latest-caselaw-343-del)
- PakistanLawsite claims proprietary rights in "the format, layout and data structures": "Pakistanlawsite, including the format, layout and data structures, is the exclusive property of" [the company]. — [Pakistanlawsite](https://www.pakistanlawsite.com/)

### Inferences (analysis)
- Headnotes, case summaries, editorial notes, "cases referred" lists, subject indexes and classification, digests and annotations are original literary or compilation work by the publisher. They are almost certainly protected under the Ordinance's general provisions on literary works and compilations (I did not separately extract those definitions in this session), and they fall **outside** s.57(1)(q)(ii), which covers only "judgment or order". **A product must not copy publishers' headnotes or editorial matter**, including PLD, SCMR, CLC, YLR, PCrLJ, MLD, PLC, PTD, CLD, NLR and PLJ. It should write its own summaries from the court's text.
- Reporter citations such as "PLD 2019 SC 1" are short factual identifiers. Citing them is very unlikely to infringe, and citation is standard practice. The legal risk lies in *bulk harvesting* those citations, or the publishers' page numbers ("star paging"), from a publisher's database. That raises copyright-in-compilation and contract (terms of service) issues. I found no Pakistani authority on pagination. Treat it as an open question and prefer court-issued identifiers (see Q4).
- Using a publisher's copy-edited judgment text is risky even where the underlying judgment is free, because of the Indian *Modak* line of reasoning and because of publishers' terms of service. Source text from the courts.
- The Peshawar High Court petition, if decided, may say something about the status of private law reports under the Law Reports Act 1875 and Article 94 QSO. A product that displays court-sourced text should note that law reports, not raw website copies, are what attract the Article 94 presumption. This is my inference from the petition as reported.

### Gaps
- No Pakistani case on copyright in headnotes or law reports was found. Searches of Pakistani databases (PLD and SCMR indexes) for "headnote" and "copyright" were not possible because those databases are behind paywalls.
- I could not verify the *Modak* holdings from the primary source (both hosts refused the connection).
- The outcome of the Peshawar High Court footnote petition after Oct 2025 is unknown.

## Q3. Publisher terms, pricing and coverage (for comparison)

### Takeaway
PakistanLawsite (PLD Publishers / Oratier Technologies) is the dominant subscription database. Its terms prohibit copying, scraping, downloading and storing, and a subscription is for a single user. A free advocate-run site (pakistancaselaw.com, formerly pakcaselaw.duckdns.org) offers reported judgments from 1970 onward but forbids bulk access and AI-training crawlers. I did not research Pakistan Law Journal (PLJ), Manzoor Law Book House, or a Pakistani "Lawnet" in this session.

- Both incumbents prohibit automated harvesting, one by contract and one by policy. A legal-AI product should not scrape either. Even the free site's content appears to be OCR of *reported* judgments, which may include publisher headnotes or pagination. That inherits the Q2 risk and is unsuitable as a source.
- The market price point is about Rs 3,000 per month per single user (PakistanLawsite, Nov 2026). That gives a comparison point for pricing.

### Gaps
- Pakistan Law Journal (PLJ), Manzoor Law Book House, NLR (National Law Reporter), CLD publishers, "Lawnet" or other Pakistani services, and other commercial platforms (for example any 2025–26 Pakistani legal-AI start-ups) were not researched in this session because of the tool budget.
- PakistanLawsite's total judgment count is not published on its homepage.

## Q4. Citation formats and neutral citation

### Takeaway
Pakistani practice centres on reporter citations in the form [Reporter] [Year] [Court] [Page], for example PLD 2019 SC 1, with parallel citations across reporters. Third-party listings show court-style identifiers such as "2025 SCP 279" for the Supreme Court and "2023 LHC 3363" for the Lahore High Court, used alongside reporter citations. **I could not find the official instrument, or its date, by which the Supreme Court or any High Court adopted a neutral citation.** That remains unresolved.

### Cited Findings (raw facts and quotes)
- A third-party index lists Supreme Court judgments with "SCP" citations, for example "2025 SCP 279", and "2025 SCP 86, 2025 SCMR 851", and "2025 SCP 32, 2025 SCMR 564, PLJ 2025 SC 236", and counts 2,116 judgments under "SCP" (snippet only). — [pakcaselaw SCP listing](https://pakcaselaw.duckdns.org/browse/journal/SCP?p=20)
- The same site lists Lahore High Court judgments with references such as "2023 LHC 3363" and "2025 LHC 239", for example "2023 LHC 1228, PLJ 2023 Cr.C. 814, 2024 YLR 1082" (snippet only). — [pakcaselaw LHC listing](https://pakcaselaw.duckdns.org/browse/court/lahore-high-court?p=675); [pakcaselaw 2023](https://pakcaselaw.duckdns.org/browse/year/2023?p=39)
- Reporter abbreviations listed by the publisher: "PLD, SCMR, CLC, PCrLJ, PTD, PLC, CLD, YLR & GBLR". — [Pakistanlawsite](https://www.pakistanlawsite.com/)
- An "extended" web search for a Pakistani neutral-citation instrument returned only Indian material: the Supreme Court of India's INSC and High Court neutral citations from 2023. No Pakistani notification surfaced. — e.g. [LiveLaw (India)](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/top-stories/supreme-court-cji-dy-chandrachud-neutral-citations-judgements-222296)

### Inferences (analysis)
- "SCP" and "LHC" identifiers appear to be numbers the courts themselves assign, probably printed on judgments uploaded to the court websites. That is consistent with neutral citation, but **unverified**. "SCP" could alternatively be a reporter abbreviation. A product should store every parallel citation and treat the court-issued identifier, where present, as the primary key once verified.

### Gaps
- I did not find the official notification, practice direction or date of adoption for "SCP" (Supreme Court) or "LHC" (Lahore High Court) identifiers. The same applies to Islamabad, Sindh, Peshawar and Balochistan High Courts. Next steps: fetch supremecourt.gov.pk and lhc.gov.pk judgment pages directly, and check one recent judgment PDF header for the identifier.

## Q5. Open data and access-to-justice initiatives

### Takeaway
As of Nov 2026, no official, bulk-downloadable open-data release of Pakistani judgments exists. Digitisation efforts focus on case management, e-filing, certified copies, analytics dashboards and AI governance. The Sindh High Court runs a public case-law portal. The LJCP posts selected "Supreme Court Important Judgments" PDFs and hosts the NJPMC's "National Guidelines for the Use of Artificial Intelligence in Judicial Institutions". Courts appear to be "public bodies" under the federal RTI Act 2017, but that is supported only by snippets.

### Cited Findings (raw facts and quotes)
- **LJCP site** (fetched 12 Nov 2026): it offers "Supreme Court Important Judgments" as PDF links, with **no judgments database**. NJPMC materials include "National Judicial Policy", "The Justice Committees - Provincial and ICT", and "National Guidelines for the Use of Artificial Intelligence in Judicial Institutions." The projects section lists a "Bi-Annual Judicial Statistics Report". There is no open-data portal. Notice: "Copyrights © 2026 All Rights Reserved by Law & Justice Commission of Pakistan." — [LJCP](https://ljcp.gov.pk/)
- **LJCP–NITB MoU** (Dawn, 2 Oct 2025): the "Law and Justice Commission of Pakistan (LJCP) and National Information Technology Board (NITB)" signed an MoU to "promote digital transformation in the justice sector". ## Q6. Data protection: anonymising victims and juveniles, and the Personal Data Protection Bill

### Takeaway
Statutes make it an offence to publish identifying details of juveniles (JJSA 2018, s.13) and of rape victims (s.376A PPC, from 2016; Anti-Rape (Investigation and Trial) Act 2021, s.26). Crucially, JJSA s.13 contains an **Explanation exempting publication of High Court and Supreme Court judgments**. That exemption does not cover trial-court or Juvenile Court judgments. The Lahore High Court in 2024 directed trial judges to use acronyms for victims. As of the latest sources (Aug–Oct 2026), Pakistan **has no enacted** Personal Data Protection Act.

### Cited Findings (raw facts and quotes)
- **Juvenile Justice System Act, 2018 (Act No. XXI of 2018), s.13 "Disclosure of identity of the juvenile."** Verbatim from the PDF; the second quote's "bfeither" is a typo in the source PDF (read "of either"):
  - s.13(1): "(1) whoever prints or publishes the name or any matter which may make known identity of a juvenile shall be punished with imprisonment bfeither description for a term which may extend to three years and shall also be liable to fine."
  - s.13(2) exceptions: (a) "by or under the order in writing of the officer-in-charge of the police station or the police officer making the investigation into such offence acting in good faith for the purposes of such investigation"; (b) "by or with the authorization in writing of the juvenile or the next-of-kin of the juvenile", with a proviso limiting next-of-kin authorisation to the head of a "recognised welfare institution or organization".
  - s.13(3): "Whoever prints or publishes any matter in relation to any proceedings before a Juvenile Court with respect to a juvenile referred to in sub-section (1) without the previous permission of Juvenile Court shall be punished with imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to two years and shall also be liable to fine."
  - Explanation: "The printing or publication of the judgment of any High Court or the Supreme Court does not amount to an offence within the meaning of this section."
  - s.25 repeals the Juvenile Justice System Ordinance, 2000 (XXII of 2000).
  - Source: [JJSA 2018 PDF (ADB LPR)](https://lpr.adb.org/sites/default/files/2024-07/pakistan-juvenile-justice-system-act-2018.pdf)
- **s.376A PPC**, inserted by the Criminal Law (Amendment) Act 2016 (Act XLIV of 2016): it criminalises printing or publishing a name or matter that may make known the identity of a rape victim, with up to three years' imprisonment and a fine. The Gazette scan has an Explanation beginning "Printing or publication of judgment of any High…", but the OCR is too damaged to confirm the rest (snippet only). — [Gazette scan (sld.com.pk)](https://sld.com.pk/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/1485428121_999.pdf); [Tribune, IHC petition](https://tribune.com.pk/story/1625956/ihc-asked-bar-media-disclosing-identity-victims)
- **Anti-Rape (Investigation and Trial) Act 2021, s.26** (as reported): prohibits disclosing the identity of a victim or the victim's family without prior written permission. **LHC judgment by Justice Tariq Saleem Sheikh (Dawn, 21 Apr 2024):** the court observed that s.26 does not directly govern court judgments but directed: "Henceforth, they shall identify the victims using acronyms rather than full names." Judges should "exercise extreme caution when writing judgments." The registrar was told to circulate the judgment to all district and sessions judges in Punjab. The underlying conviction was under s.377-B PPC. — [Dawn, 21 Apr 2024](https://dawn.com/news/1828670/lhc-asks-judges-to-protect-rape-victims-identity-in-verdicts)
- In 2026, Punjab's prosecutor general reportedly issued guidelines under s.376-A to protect rape victims' identities (snippet only). — [Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2507030/protection-of-rape-victims-identity-stressed?amp=1)
- **Personal Data Protection Bill status:**
  - The 2023 bill received Federal Cabinet approval in late July 2023 and was "headed towards votes in the National Assembly and Senate" (snippet only). — [Biometric Update, Aug 2023](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202308/pakistan-data-protection-bill-approaches-vote-and-draws-criticism-from-privacy-international)
  - A Dawn piece says the bill has sat in draft since 2018 (snippet only). — [Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/2021159)
  - An Express Tribune op-ed titled "Pakistan can no longer wait for personal data protection law" (snippet only). — [Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2625470/pakistan-can-no-longer-wait-for-personal-data-protection-law)
  - Caveat: the "Aug/Oct 2026" labels on the Tribune results are page-render ("Today's paper") dates and **may not be publication dates**.
  - MoITT released a draft **National Data Governance Policy (June 2026)** for consultation; per the snippet, consultation closed 10 July 2026. — [MoITT PDF](https://moitt.gov.pk/SiteImage/Policies/Data Governance Policy June 2026.pdf); [Profit, 30 Jun 2026](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2026/06/30/govt-seeks-feedback-on-draft-data-governance-policy-2026)
  - No report of the bill passing the National Assembly in 2026 was found.

### Inferences (analysis)
- Republishing **High Court and Supreme Court** judgments that name a juvenile does not offend JJSA s.13 (Explanation). It is unclear whether s.376A PPC has an equivalent explanation; the Indian counterpart, s.228A IPC, does. Republishing **trial-court, Juvenile Court or Sessions Court** judgments or orders that identify a juvenile or a rape victim carries criminal exposure. That exposure covers up to three years under JJSA s.13(1) and s.376A, and up to two years under s.13(3) for publishing Juvenile Court proceedings without permission.
- A product therefore needs an anonymisation pipeline for names, addresses, family details and photos of victims and juveniles, at least for subordinate-court material. It should also be applied as best practice to superior-court judgments in sexual-offence, juvenile and family cases, consistent with the LHC's 2024 acronym direction and with the s.57(1)(q)(ii) proviso where courts restrict publication.
- No general data-protection statute applies yet (as of the latest sources). Sectoral rules and PECA 2016 still apply but were not researched here. The product should be designed to comply with the 2023/2025 bill drafts in case they are enacted.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm the verbatim text of s.376A PPC, including whether its Explanation exempts High Court and Supreme Court judgments. Fetch the official PPC text from pakistancode.gov.pk.
- I did not obtain the verbatim text of s.26 of the Anti-Rape (Investigation and Trial) Act 2021. The account above is from Dawn's report.
- I did not research family-law confidentiality rules, such as in-camera proceedings under the Family Courts Act 1964, or any provincial child-protection laws, for example the KP Child Protection and Welfare Act 2010, which Dawn cited for a "s.23(2)" media bar.
- I could not confirm the precise current stage of the Personal Data Protection Bill (latest version name, cabinet re-approval, NA introduction) from primary sources such as na.gov.pk or moitt.gov.pk.
